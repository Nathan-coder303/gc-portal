9. Structural Suitability and Material Weight

9.1 Understanding Dead Loads. Dead load is the permanent weight of building materials. Roofing materials differ greatly in weight; asphalt shingles typically weigh 2.5–4.5 pounds per square foot, whereas clay or concrete tiles weigh roughly 9–15 pounds per square foot. Three layers of asphalt shingles weigh approximately 690 pounds per square (100 square feet).

9.2 Structural Evaluation Requirement. Because clay and concrete tiles can weigh three to four times more than asphalt shingles, switching from shingles to tiles may overload existing framing. The Florida Building Code requires that buildings be designed to support the imposed dead and live loads. Customers contemplating a change from shingles to tile, or the addition of heavy equipment (such as solar panels), must obtain a structural evaluation by a licensed engineer at their own expense. MIBH Construction is not a licensed engineer and offers no opinion on structural capacity. Work will not proceed with heavier materials until the Customer provides written evidence that the roof structure can safely support the increased load.

9.3 Release of Liability for Structural Capacity. The Customer assumes all responsibility for the roof's structural capacity. If the Customer elects to install heavier roofing materials or add weight‑bearing attachments without engineering certification, the Customer agrees to indemnify and hold MIBH Construction harmless from any claims, damages or losses arising from structural failure. Any required reinforcement or structural modifications will be treated as a change order and priced separately.
